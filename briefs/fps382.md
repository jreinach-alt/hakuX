## Evidence so far
Run 0-0-y-1790411511-titlebench-9r, ref 6561442869, 240 s hands-off. Median gfps per 30 s bucket:
18, 9.5, 9, 9, 9, 29, 29, 19, 23.5. Every frame is the intro FMV, decoded cleanly, no crash lines. The dip to 9 lasts
~120 s and the 29 fps stretch is clean, so the drop is scene-dependent, not a constant cost.
Related, not the same: lane.blinx372 (PR #374, docs/investigations/perf-blinx-372.md) split Blinx's frame into two
synchronous GPU-completion waits; lane.blinx372b (PR #381) is naming the SURFACE_DOWN site. Reuse its tooling
(docs/lanes/blinx372*/workread.py, sites.py, the perflog `hakuX-stall` / `hakuX-pace` lines) once #381 folds;
until then read the perflog directly.

## Goal
1. Separate content from cost. An FMV presents decoded frames on the game's schedule, so a low gfps may be the video's
   own rate. Compare the same intro on the desktop hakuX build (frame timestamps, not the overlay) or against silicon
   if a capture exists. State the video's native present rate.
2. If the emulator is the cost: one perflog soak of the intro on the Nova (master apk), find the 9 fps window, and read
   renderer-thread busy %, GPU ms, recording ms, and the stall counters per site for it versus the 29 fps window.
3. Name the resource that differs between the two windows (a synchronous download, a YUV/overlay path, a texture
   upload, audio/Sofdec CPU on the guest side) and price a fix, or say the FMV is content-paced.

## Falsifier
"The 9 fps window is content-paced": the video's own present interval in that window is <= 111 ms with the guest
idle-waiting for the next frame (renderer busy < 60%). If renderer busy is >= 90% in the 9 fps window and not in the 29 fps
window, the emulator is the cost. Register this before the soak.
