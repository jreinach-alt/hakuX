# Issue #10 is not one rule: the bump arithmetic is already right

Measured against today's Adreno sweep (`fb4dfafc6d38`, captures written
2026-09-12 13:57, goldens `/home/justin/goldens/results`). 82 captures,
818,008 structural px, complete golden coverage. Reproduced offline from the
PNGs with `docs/testing/bump_decompose.py`, which returns the scoreboard's
own figures exactly -- 818,008 structural, 2,386,612 differing, 1,568,604
off-by-one -- so the numbers below are the same numbers the board ranks on.

**The headline, and it is a negative.** #10 was on the board as the largest
fully-covered failure and read as "an entire subsystem is failing". It is not.
The bump matrix, scale and offset arithmetic is accurate to a fifth of a pixel
on a 5.25 px feature. 40 of the 82 captures contain **no bump error at all**.
The 818,008 px is four disjoint classes, three of which are texture-input
paths and one of which belongs to #38.

## Measured: 40 captures carry one byte-identical mask that is not ours

Exactly 40 of the 82 captures sit at **precisely 1,576 structural px**, and
**all 40 masks are byte-identical** -- not similar, identical, checked
elementwise. The mask is 11 horizontal rows at

```
76, 97, 118, 139, 160, 181, 202,   256, 277, 298, 319
```

a 21-row period with one 54-row step where the quad row changes, 316 columns
wide, 255/224/143/101/5/3/1 and 316/274/148/106 px per row. That is the
texture-coordinate interpolation class shared with `Texture_render_target`,
`Texture_palette` and `Texture_wrap_mode`, tracked under **#38**. It is
format-independent because it does not come from the texture.

It is also contained whole (1,576/1,576) inside the YUV, `BumpEnvLum_R16B16`
and `DXT1` masks, and partially inside the rest, which is why per-capture
counts like 3,895 decompose as 1,544 floor + 2,351 residual.

The 40: `BumpMap_A8`, `_A8_L`, `_A8Y8`, `_AY8`, `_AY8_L`, `_R8B8`,
`_R16B16_L`, `_Y8`, `_Y8_L` and 31 `BumpEnvLum_*` captures (every one except
`R16B16`, `R16B16_L`, `UYVY_L`, `YUY2_L`, `DXT1`, and the four `_B`/`_R90`
variants). **Nothing in #10 to fix for any of them.**

## Measured: the displacement the bump stage applies is correct

The `Bump_map` goldens hold **exactly two colours** wherever we are
structurally wrong -- `(254,0,0,255)` opaque red and `(33,32,32,191)`
half-alpha grey -- in all 40 captures, and our own frames hold the same two.
Whole-frame palette counts already agree: `BumpMap_R16B16` has ours
red 63,377 / grey 48,119 against gold 63,229 / 48,267, a difference of 148 px,
while 32,748 px are in the wrong place. **Counts match; placement does not.**
Any rule fitted to histograms or sorted populations here would fit noise --
this is the same degeneracy that cost an afternoon on #41 and forced today's
"sorted populations match" retraction.

So the informative quantity is not a pixel count but the **position of the
red/grey boundary**, which is a direct readout of the applied displacement:
TEX1 is a fixed checkerboard, so a cell edge lands where `pT + dsdt` crosses
a cell line. `docs/testing/bump_edge_shift.py` measures it, matching each
golden edge to the nearest of ours per row.

The checker cell period comes out at **5 px measured**, against 168/32 = 5.25
px predicted from the test source (256-texel texture, 8-texel cells,
texcoord1 spanning 0..1 over a 168 px quad). That agreement is the method's
validation gate.

| group | mean dx | mean abs dx | edges placed exactly |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| floor-only (`A8`, `Y8`, `AY8`, `A8Y8`, `R8B8`, `R16B16_L`) | +0.002..-0.002 | 0.006-0.014 | **99.5%** |
| `DXT1`, `G8B8_B_R90` | +0.015 | 0.021 | 98.1% |
| `A4R4G4B4`, `R6G5B5`, `G8B8_B` | -0.068..+0.047 | 0.068-0.076 | 93-95% |
| `G8B8_R90` | -0.059 | 0.095 | 95.3% |
| `A1R5G5B5`, `X1R5G5B5`, `R5G6B5` | -0.036..-0.061 | 0.104-0.110 | 89.8% |
| 8-bit RGB family (12 captures) | +0.076 | 0.224 | 89.7% |
| `R16B16_B` | +0.005 | 0.659 | 57.1% |
| `R16B16` | -0.024 | 1.433 | 71.4% (+2,924 unmatched) |
| `Y16`, `Y16_L` | **-2.779** | **3.307** | **33.4%** (+1,927 unmatched) |

A wrong matrix entry, scale or offset displaces the boundary by whole cells.
Everything above `R16B16_B` sits inside a quarter of a pixel of the hardware,
on a 5.25 px feature -- that is agreement to 4% of a cell. **The BUMPENVMAP
dS/dT/matrix path is not where the remaining error is.**

### The one localised exception, and it is not what it looks like

In the 12 eight-bit-RGB captures the 19,914 golden edges break down as 17,870
exact, 1,331 at -1 px, and **325 at +4 plus 325 at +5**. The +4/+5 group is
not scattered: it is two boundaries, at golden columns 188/189 and 368/369,
confined to rows 246..413.

Those are 42 px into each of the two *bottom* quads (146+42, 326+42). The
bottom quads are the `ysigned=1` pair, where the G channel -- the dT source --
is flagged signed; and u = 42/168 = 0.25 is exactly where the bilinear ramp
across the bump texture's x=2 seam begins, since texcoord0 spans texels 1..3
and the G byte steps 0x7f -> 0x80 at texel 2.

**Caveat on my own method, stated because it changes the reading:** a vertical
boundary moving horizontally normally means a dS error, but here it does not.
With `mat11 = 0.5`, dT sweeping +0.99 -> -1.0 across that ramp moves the
vertical coordinate by +-16 cells. An even/odd cell-parity flip from a tiny
dT error re-colours the cell, and the edge detector reports that as the
boundary "moving". So these 650 edges are a **dT precision effect in the
signed sweep**, not a horizontal displacement -- consistent with the issue's
existing note that the signed-sweep quadrants sit within a sub-texel of the
hardware's filter arithmetic at about 2.3k px per quadrant (measured here:
2,351 px residual per capture, 97% of it in runs of 1-2 px across 16
columns).

## Rival rules, scored

`docs/testing/bump_oracle.py` is a host-side forward model of the generic
BUMPENVMAP path built from the test literals (nxdk_pgraph_tests @`33e7c6b`):
four 168x168 quads at (146,66)/(326,66)/(146,246)/(326,246), bump texture
`{0x007F4500, 0x00804500, ...}` as RGBA8888, texcoord0 over texels 1..3,
`SetBumpEnv(0.3, 0.0, 0.0, 0.5)` on stage 1, an A8R8G8B8 checkerboard of
8-texel cells (`0xFF0000FE`/`0x7F202122`), NEAREST, then
SRC_ALPHA/ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA over the `0xFE202020` clear.

Its integer blend independently reproduces both golden colours -- 127/255 over
254/255 gives 190.749 -> **191** and `(33,32,32)` -- which is the first gate it
had to pass.

Scored on `BumpMap_A8R8G8B8`, 111,496 covered px:

| channel assignment | rounding | wrong | % exact |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **dS=B dT=G (psh.c today)** | over128, round | **21,839** | **80.41%** |
| dS=B dT=G | over128, no round | 22,236 | 80.06% |
| dS=B dT=G | over127 clamped | 24,177 | 78.32% |
| dS=G dT=B | over127 | 50,761 | 54.47% |
| dS=G dT=B | over128 | 52,399 | 53.00% |
| dS=G dT=R | over128 | 67,466 | 39.49% |
| **dS=R dT=G** (`pixel_shader_tests.h` formula) | over128 | **83,389** | **25.21%** |

**Rejected, measured:** the channel assignment documented in
`nxdk_pgraph_tests/src/tests/pixel_shader_tests.h:30` as
`u' = u + BUMPENVMAT00 * normalmap.r + BUMPENVMAT10 * normalmap.g` -- reading
the offsets from R and G -- scores 25.21% against 80.41% for the B and G that
`psh.c` uses. Hardware really does take dS from blue. That corroborates the
correction already recorded in `bump-yuv-source.md` and closes it with a
score rather than an argument.

**Rejected, measured:** a `/127` or unrounded reading of the offset byte, both
worse than the `/128`-with-rounding rule at `psh.c:1460`.

**Not settled, and I will not claim it is.** The model's own floor is 19.6%
where the emulator's is 3.5%, because dT moves the sample by +-16 cells across
the signed sweep, so sub-byte differences in the filter weights flip cell
parity wholesale. The oracle discriminates *gross* rules (a factor of three
between channel assignments) and must not be used to settle rounding: the
0.35-point gap between "round" and "no round" is well inside its own error.

## Falsifiable predictions a correct fix must reproduce

Per-capture figures are in
`docs/testing/run-2026-09-12-bump-decomposition.tsv` (82 rows: structural,
floor overlap, non-floor residual, golden colours inside the structural mask,
alpha figures) and `docs/testing/run-2026-09-12-bump-edge-shift.tsv` (38 rows:
edge counts, signed and absolute displacement, fraction placed exactly).

The prediction that separates "right palette, wrong counts" from "rule not
found":

1. **Every one of the 82 captures must land on exactly 1,576 structural px**
   once its class is fixed -- not 0. The #38 floor is byte-identical across
   40 captures and independent of the bump path, so it survives any bump fix.
   A capture that reaches 0 means #38 was fixed too; a capture that stops
   between its current value and 1,576 means the class was only partly found.
2. The 40 already at 1,576 **must not move**. They are the regression gate.
3. `BumpMap_R16B16` must go 32,748 -> 1,576 with red 63,377 -> **63,229** and
   grey 48,119 -> **48,267** (a 148 px swing, which is why counts alone cannot
   confirm it -- the edge displacement must go 1.433 -> under 0.02 px and
   the 2,924 unmatched golden edges must fall to 0).
4. `BumpMap_R16B16_B` 35,106 -> 1,576, mean abs dx 0.659 -> under 0.02.
5. `BumpMap_Y16` and `_Y16_L` 22,374 -> 1,576 each, mean dx **-2.779 -> 0**,
   edges placed exactly 33.4% -> 99%+, unmatched 1,927 -> 0.
6. `BumpEnvLum_R16B16` and `_R16B16_L` 50,665 -> 1,576 each. These two are the
   only captures in the 82 whose golden holds many colours (115) where we are
   structurally wrong, so they are the only ones that can confirm a rule
   rather than merely fail to contradict it.
7. The four YUV captures 111,496 -> 1,576 each. The golden's two colours there
   are `(16,84,16)` and `(72,255,18)` for `BumpMap`, `(16,143,16)` and
   `(16,85,16)` for `BumpEnvLum` -- **green**, where ours are red and grey.
   A fix must produce green; any candidate that merely redistributes our two
   reds is wrong before it is scored.
8. `Bump_env_lum`'s 1,568,604 off-by-one px are **not** in scope and must not
   be expected to move.

## Which goldens can discriminate, and which cannot

The corpus discrimination sheet `run-2026-09-12-golden-discrimination.tsv`
reports 85-118 golden colours for many `Bump_env_lum` captures. **That is
counted over the whole differing mask, which in those captures is 97%
off-by-one.** Counted inside the structural mask -- the pixels a bump rule
would move -- the picture inverts:

- **Can discriminate (2 captures):** `BumpEnvLum_R16B16` and `_R16B16_L`, 115
  golden colours over 50,665 structural px.
- **Weakly (4):** `BumpEnvLum_X8R8G8B8_B` (19 colours / 3,005 px),
  `_R6G5B5_B` (17 / 2,408), `_X8R8G8B8_B_R90` (34 / 961), `_R6G5B5_B_R90`
  (33 / 635).
- **Cannot (all 40 `Bump_map` captures, and 16 of 42 `Bump_env_lum`):** the
  golden holds 1 or 2 colours inside the structural mask. A two-colour golden
  says only which side of a boundary a pixel fell. It cannot fit a matrix, a
  scale or an offset.

The 20 `Bump_env_lum` captures with 66-85 structural golden colours are all at
the 1,576 floor (checked: every one of them): the colour variety is in #38's
boundary pixels crossing the luminance ramp, not in a bump error. So of the 82
captures, **2 can confirm a bump rule, 4 can weakly, and 76 can only fail to
contradict one.**

**Consequence for whoever takes the R16B16 work:** fit against
`BumpEnvLum_R16B16`, not `BumpMap_R16B16`. The prior attempts recorded in the
issue were scored against `BumpMap_R16B16`/`_B`, whose goldens hold two
colours -- which is why two plausible mappings could both be tried and both
come back "worse" without the measurement saying anything about why.

## A correction to the issue's recorded reasoning

Issue #10's second comment concludes: "`BumpMap_R16B16_L` uses the ordinary
`BUMPENVMAP` path with the same format and sits at the 1,576 floor, so the
R16B16 decode, swizzle and sampling are all correct."

**`_L` does not mean luminance. It means linear.**
`bump_map_tests.cpp:304` appends `_L` when `texture_format.xbox_linear`, so
`BumpMap_R16B16_L` is `LU_IMAGE_R16B16` -- a *different format* from the
swizzled `SZ_R16B16` in `BumpMap_R16B16`. The working case does not exercise
the swizzle at all, so "the swizzle is correct" does not follow from it.

What the captures actually say, measured:

| capture | format | stage program | structural |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `BumpMap_R16B16_L` | `LU_IMAGE_R16B16` linear | BUMPENVMAP | 1,576 (floor) |
| `BumpMap_R16B16` | `SZ_R16B16` swizzled | DOT_PRODUCT + DOT_ST | 32,748 |
| `BumpMap_R16B16_B` | `SZ_R16B16` swizzled | DOT_PRODUCT + DOT_ST | 35,106 |
| `BumpEnvLum_R16B16` | `SZ_R16B16` swizzled | BUMPENVMAP_LUM | 50,665 |
| `BumpEnvLum_R16B16_L` | `LU_IMAGE_R16B16` linear | BUMPENVMAP_LUM | 50,665 |

The two `Bump_env_lum` figures are **identical**, linear and swizzled alike.
So the failure does not track the swizzle; it tracks the stage program.
R16B16 is correct through plain BUMPENVMAP and wrong through
BUMPENVMAP_LUM and through DOT_ST. BUMPENVMAP_LUM is the only one of the
three that reads a **third** channel -- the luminance, taken from component 0
(R) at `psh.c:2065` -- and `R16B16` is the only two-component 16-bit format in
the set. `R8B8` and `G8B8`, two-component but 8-bit, are both at the floor in
`Bump_env_lum`. That is where the R16B16 luminance work should start.

## The 6.46M alpha signal: separate, and already gone

Not explained by any rule here, because on this hardware it does not exist.
Measured across all 82 of today's Adreno captures:

- **`Bump_env_lum`: 0 alpha-differing pixels in all 42 captures.** The 6.46M
  px block was lavapipe's blend store (ours 190, gold 191, one distinct pair),
  settled in `bump-alpha-blend-rounding.md` and confirmed here.
- **`Bump_map`: 40 captures do differ in alpha, but `alpha_only` is 0 in all
  82** -- every alpha-differing pixel also differs in RGB, and the count
  equals the structural count exactly in every row (3,895/3,895,
  1,576/1,576, 32,748/32,748). The pairs are (255,191) and (191,255) with
  max delta 64.

255 and 191 are the alpha of the opaque red cell and of the half-alpha grey
cell. So `Bump_map`'s alpha difference is the checker cell *identity*
swapping across a displaced boundary, arithmetically downstream of the colour
error and carrying no independent information. **There is no alpha rule to
find.**

## Negatives, recorded

1. **dS from R (the `pixel_shader_tests.h` formula): rejected**, 25.21%
   against 80.41%.
2. **`/127` and unrounded offset readings: rejected**, both worse than the
   current `/128`-with-rounding.
3. **`dot_map` nibble decode: correct, hypothesis dead.** `Test16bit` pushes
   `NV097_SET_DOT_RGBMAPPING = 0x044` intending `dotmap_hilo_1` on stages 1
   and 2. `psh.c:2551-2553` takes `dot_map[1]` from bits 0-3, `dot_map[2]`
   from bits 4-7, `dot_map[3]` from bits 8-11, giving 4, 4, 0 -- exactly the
   test's intent. I went looking for an off-by-one nibble here and there
   isn't one.
4. **"Our R16B16 output is flat where the golden ramps": wrong, retracted
   before it left this document.** The top-6 colour pairs for
   `BumpEnvLum_R16B16` all have ours as `(27,27,27)`, which reads as a
   collapsed output. Counted properly over the structural mask, ours holds
   **118** colours against the golden's 115. Ours is not flat; the top-N
   pair list was misleading. This is exactly why the method rule says regions
   and populations, not the head of a sorted list.
5. **The whole-frame histogram is not a discriminator** for any `Bump_map`
   capture: red and grey counts already agree with the goldens to within
   0.3%, while up to 32,748 px are misplaced.

## An unmeasured code lead, flagged as inference

`dotmap_hilo_1` at `psh.c:1507` builds its 16-bit halves with
`uint(col.a * float(0xff)) << 8 | uint(col.r * float(0xff))`. `uint()`
truncates. Every other converter in this file rounds -- `bump_signed`,
`bump_snorm`, `bump_unsigned` and `signed_channel` all call `round()`, and
the comment at `psh.c:1451` records that the rounding was itself derived from
the goldens. A filtered UNORM8 sample that should be an exact integer arrives
a hair below it, and truncation then drops it by one.

`dotmap_hilo_hemisphere` at `psh.c:1528` has the same construction.

**This is inference from reading the code, not a measurement.** I could not
score it: it only affects the `Test16bit` captures, whose goldens hold two
colours, and the oracle does not cover the DOT_PRODUCT/DOT_ST path. It is
cheap to try and worth trying, but it must not be written up as the rule
until a run scores it.

## Caveats

- Both bump suites ran on a **shared disc** (`z-sweep-009`, `z-sweep-010`
  `run1.log`: "ran on a shared disc, not one test per run ... contamination
  between their own tests is not ruled out"). Every figure here is
  single-run. Per `device-run-flakes.md` a lone score change wants two reruns
  before it is believed; the identical repeats across format pairs
  (`_A8R8G8B8` and `_A8R8G8B8_L` agreeing to the pixel, 12 captures sharing
  3,895) argue these are stable, but they are not independently confirmed.
- Captures are from today 13:57 against `fb4dfafc6d38`, checked by mtime, and
  the offline decomposition reproduces the sweep's own totals exactly. The
  on-device `bump_info.txt` / `bump_vram.bin` / `decoded_*.bin` artefacts
  dated 2026-09-08 were **not** used: four days old, and nothing here needed
  them.
- The oracle covers the generic BUMPENVMAP path only, at a 19.6% self-error on
  `BumpMap_A8R8G8B8` and 46.4% on `BumpMap_G8B8_R90` (where `mat10 = 0.3`
  puts dT on the S axis and the parity sensitivity doubles). It is a
  rival-ranking tool, not a reference renderer, and the R90 figure is the
  clearest warning not to read absolute scores off it -- only the ordering
  between rivals on the same capture.
