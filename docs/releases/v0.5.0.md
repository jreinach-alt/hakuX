# v0.5.0

The first release rated by play rather than by pixel counts alone. 52 titles
reach gameplay and hold it — a timed run with no crash, hang, audio dropout or
hitch, at 30 fps or better for at least 90% of it — on a Retroid Pocket Nova
or an AYN Thor. They are listed, with exactly what the rating means, in
[`docs/COMPATIBILITY.md`](https://github.com/jreinach-alt/hakuX/blob/master/docs/COMPATIBILITY.md).
It also brings save states, several games folders, a settings page for the
renderer's faster paths, and the rendering-accuracy work begun in
[v0.4.0-j1](https://github.com/jreinach-alt/hakuX/blob/master/docs/releases/v0.4.0-j1.md).

**Three things to know before installing:**

- It needs **Android 10 or later and an ARMv8.2 CPU with LSE atomics**
  (Snapdragon 845 / 865 class or newer). v0.4.x installed on Android 8. On an
  older ARMv8.0 part such as the Snapdragon 835, 660, 665 or 460, the app now
  shows an "Unsupported CPU" message and exits instead of crashing.
- It installs **alongside** an official hakuX build rather than replacing it,
  and upgrades v0.4.1-j1 or any earlier fork build in place. Nothing is
  uninstalled and no saves are at risk.
- Launching it from ES-DE still needs the two configuration files in
  [`docs/es-de/`](https://github.com/jreinach-alt/hakuX/tree/master/docs/es-de).

### New

- **Save states.** Ten slots per game (0 to 9) and an auto slot, saved and
  loaded from the pause menu, with undo for the last save and the last load.
  Optional auto-save on quit, auto-load at launch, and compression (about
  10 MB a state instead of 42 MB) are under Settings, then Save States. States
  are files on the device, outside the Xbox hard disk image. The default
  folder is removed when the app is uninstalled; choose a folder there to keep
  them.
- **Several games folders.** Add the SD card and internal storage, or any
  other folder, in Settings; the library shows all of them together.
- **Ubershader** (Settings, then Graphics; on by default, with a per-game
  override). A new scene draws at once with a general shader while its exact
  shader compiles, instead of freezing until it is ready (Kabuki Warriors,
  Dead or Alive 3, Tron 2.0). It can cost frame rate in some games: ToeJam &
  Earl III ran at 54 fps instead of 60 in testing. A banner says so when it is
  off.
- **Pipelines pre-built from a previous play.** A game you have played before
  no longer freezes the first time a scene loads: the pipelines it built last
  time are pre-built on a compile pool, and the pipeline cache is saved and
  reloaded. (Arctic Thunder's 43 pipeline creates had cost 4,661 ms on a
  cold start.)
- **Fast renderer paths** (Settings, then Graphics; on by default, with a
  per-game override). Faster report queries, texture scans, draw recording,
  shader uniform updates, and hand-off of GPU commands from the emulated CPU.
  (Need for Speed: Most Wanted's warm race-start countdown: 44.23 → 34.22 ms a
  frame, with no correctness regression across 37 Playable titles screened.)
- **GPU surface reuse** (Settings, then Graphics; on by default, with a
  per-game override). A render surface that is reused is rebound on the GPU
  instead of recreated. (NBA Live 07: 23 → 47 fps median.)
- **The AYN Thor's bottom screen.** A game started on a screen smaller than
  1280×720, such as the Thor's bottom screen, fills it instead of a 640×480
  corner.

### Fixed

- **Need for Speed: Most Wanted.** Stuttering and repeated audio in movies
  and speech. The first race after loading starts at 29.8 fps instead of
  26.4, and the race start renders through the driver's system-memory path,
  which cuts GPU time a frame by about a quarter there.
- **GTA San Andreas.** Hard-edged white polygons in the sky.
- **RalliSport Challenge.** Rival cars vanished on alternate frames during the
  countdown and close passes. A title that gates its draws on occlusion
  queries now reads this frame's result.
- **Forza Motorsport** no longer slows down over the course of a race.
- **007: Agent Under Fire and Dead or Alive Ultimate** render through the
  system-memory path too. (Agent Under Fire: 17 → 26 fps.)
- **Titles that set their clock from VBLANK ran slow.** A deferred VBLANK
  reset the timing grid instead of holding it, so VBLANK arrived at
  56.587 Hz instead of 59.939 Hz on Galleon; the game clock lost several
  seconds a minute. Separately, a 720p or 1080i title was detected as PAL and
  timed at 50 Hz; PAL detection now applies to 481 to 576 lines only.
- **Crashes and hangs.**
  - A translation-cache bug that could hang or crash the emulator when a
    disc DMA thread raced code recompilation.
  - A crash on the first texture bind after the texture cache filled up.
  - A crash when a game sent a very large draw through the inline vertex
    path; the buffer now grows instead of overflowing.
- **The x87 `FRNDINT` instruction** honours the game's rounding mode instead
  of the host's.

### Speed

- The emulated CPU no longer empties its whole address-translation cache each
  time the GPU starts or stops watching a surface, only the pages concerned
  (Conker: 145 full flushes a second down to 1).
- Fewer wipes of the emulated CPU's jump cache. (Crimson Skies: the wipes
  took 8.6% of guest CPU time and now take none; the slowest tenth of frames
  went from 23 to 26 fps.)
- A memory-read shortcut that never engaged in gameplay is gone, and with it
  a check on every read (GTA San Andreas: about 5% less emulated-CPU work a
  frame).
- The frame limiter and the renderer's waits for the GPU sleep instead of
  spinning a CPU core.
- The native library is built for ARMv8.2: native thread-local storage,
  inline atomics, and direct calls within the library. This is the reason for
  the new CPU and Android requirements above.

On 30 fps-capped titles several of these changes do not show as frame rate;
they leave more headroom.

### Rendering

Measured, as in v0.4.0-j1, against framebuffers captured from a real Xbox
(the `nxdk_pgraph_tests` golden results). Three of v0.4.0-j1's known
residuals are closed: the YUV-to-RGB rule, DXT1 decode rounding and the
signed blend equations.

- **Wide lines** are drawn with the hardware's footprint (47.82% → 99.58%
  match against the hardware captures).
- **Signed blend equations** apply their blend factors, and **colour
  surfaces without an alpha channel** blend with the hardware's pad alpha.
- **YUV textures** (YUY2, UYVY) round each colour term separately, as the
  hardware does (`TexFmt_YUY2_L` and `TexFmt_UYVY_L`: 136,900 px each → 0).
- **DXT textures** widen colour endpoints by bit replication and round the
  interpolated colours (`Texture_DXT`: 1,053,357 → 586,700 differing px; all
  six colour-only DXT3 and DXT5 tests exact). DXT1's ordered dither saturates
  at both ends.
- **A render target sampled as a texture** of another format is read in the
  right channel order (Blend tests: 5,893,287 → 3,562,479 differing px), is
  detected reliably when a texture shares its address and size, and gets its
  barrier back when a later draw writes it.
- **Shadow-map comparisons** quantise the reference to the depth texture's
  precision (`Texture_shadow_comparator`: 236 → 260 of 288 exact; 41,716 →
  8,776 px).
- **Objects that were silently dropped** are now drawn: the 2D solid-line
  object, the clip rectangle a blit is bounded by, and polygon stipple
  patterns.
- **Blits** that run past their destination tile follow the tile address map
  (`Image_blit::BlitBeyondWidth`: 284,628 → 0 px). The AND blend blit refuses
  formats narrower than 32 bits instead of corrupting them, and rounds.
- **Smaller corrections:** float-16 depth flushes to zero at the hardware's
  threshold; the lighting switch gates a vertex program's colour outputs;
  vertex colours carry the lighting unit's 13-bit fraction and are quantised
  to a byte before interpolation; a texture coordinate exactly on a texel
  boundary resolves to the hardware's side; a degenerate cube-map reflection
  has a defined direction; back faces take the back specular colour; a vertex
  program that writes no fog coordinate inherits the last one written.

### Reporting a bug

- Issue forms for a bug and for a title or compatibility report collect the
  device, the hakuX version, the steps, and the diagnostics. See
  [`docs/reporting-bugs.md`](https://github.com/jreinach-alt/hakuX/blob/master/docs/reporting-bugs.md).
- **Debug Capture** (a frame capture for a rendering bug) is off in this
  release. To attach one to a report, switch on Settings, then Debug, then
  Debug Tools; the pause menu then shows Debug Capture.
- About links to this repository, its license and its privacy notice.

### Known residuals

- Audio DSP processing is off by default.
- Need for Speed: Most Wanted is rated Playable on a full race played by hand;
  its frame rate still dips in places.
- Many titles boot and run with visible graphical faults, and many have not
  been tried. See
  [`KNOWN_ISSUES.md`](https://github.com/jreinach-alt/hakuX/blob/master/KNOWN_ISSUES.md)
  and the [issue tracker](https://github.com/jreinach-alt/hakuX/issues).
