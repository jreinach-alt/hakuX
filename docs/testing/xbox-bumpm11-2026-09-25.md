# #10 on silicon: the m11 cut collapses, but the m11 = 5.0 image cannot show the effect, and neither could #10's own control

**Measured 2026-09-25 on the project console.** It was registered in
[`docs/lanes/xbox/bumpm11-run.md`](../lanes/xbox/bumpm11-run.md): first at
`91429aff62`, amended at `4496dc1e39` before any silicon run, with the
ruled-out worlds named at `41bc852b50`. The host routed it on #112 at 03:50Z.

**In short:**
- **By the letter,** M is COLLAPSE and C1 holds. At m11 = 5.0, silicon renders
  Y16 exactly as Y8, apart from the printed format name.
- **But that zero does not discriminate.** At m11 = 5.0 the whole quad renders
  as horizontal stripes: 150 of 168 seam-band rows are one colour. A stripe is
  unchanged by any horizontal shift, and #10's effect *is* a horizontal
  cell-parity flip. So the image cannot show it.
- **The same holds for #10's own control.** The golden `BumpEnvLum_Y16`
  (m11 = 5.0) is also all stripes. PR #187 used that capture's agreement at
  the floor to kill the "low byte of the filtered 16-bit value" rival. That
  kill is void, and the rival #10 called "the only rival with the right
  magnitude" is alive again.

5.0. The vertical term is live, too: silicon's Y8 at 0.5 against 5.0 differs on
27,904 px.

## Why the collapse does not discriminate

#10's effect is a **horizontal cell-parity flip** inside each quad's seam band
(columns 44–102): 5,336–5,398 px per quad at m11 = 0.5, every one of them in
the band. A flip like that can only show where the lookup varies along x.

| capture | seam-band rows that are a single colour (of 168, per quad) |
|---|---:|
| `BumpMap_Y8`, `BumpMap_Y16` (m11 0.5) | 0 |
| `BumpMap_Y8_m11x10`, `BumpMap_Y16_m11x10` (m11 5.0) | **150** |
| golden `BumpEnvLum_Y16` (m11 5.0) | **150** |
| golden `BumpEnvLum_A8` | 0 |

- **At m11 = 0.5,** the band samples the checkerboard. Y16 shows the finer
  flipped checker there, which is #10's effect.
- **At m11 = 5.0,** every quad renders as horizontal stripes, with each row
  one colour. It still uses the two checker colours in the same 49/48
  proportion, so the lookup is on the texture. It just does not vary with x.
- **A horizontal shift of a stripe changes nothing.** So a zero at 5.0 is
  what this image gives whether or not silicon's Y16 read differs.

**What the registration got wrong.** It said COLLAPSE "rules out a world where
silicon reads a Y16 bump source differently from Y8 whatever the matrix is".
That assumed the m11 = 5.0 image could show a horizontal flip. It cannot. The
golden that shows this (`BumpEnvLum_Y16`) was on disk when I registered, and I
did not look at it. The COLLAPSE is recorded as measured. The inference
registered for it is withdrawn.

## The control #10 relied on is blind in the same way

PR #187 killed its one right-sized rival, the low byte of the filtered 16-bit
value, with `BumpEnvLum_Y16`: it binds SZ_Y16 over the same kind of seam and
sits at the 1,576 px floor.

- **PR #187's argument for sensitivity.** It called the region "measured
  non-blind" because `BumpEnvLum_Y16` against `BumpEnvLum_A8` is 4,770 px
  there.
- **Why that doesn't show it.** A8 renders a checkerboard and Y16 renders
  stripes. Two different images differing says nothing about whether *Y16's
  own* image can show a horizontal flip, and it cannot: 150 of 168 rows are
  single-coloured.
- **So the low-byte rival is not refuted.** It stays the only named mechanism
  of the right magnitude.

## What a discriminating run needs

- **A seam band that samples the checkerboard.** Keep m11 small enough that
  the Y8 band has no single-colour rows (0.5 does). Vary the factors #10 names
  there:
  - m11 at 0.25 and 1.0, to see whether the flip pattern tracks m11;
  - the luminance values, 46/47 against 82/83;
  - the LUMINANCE stage, at a matrix that keeps the checkerboard.
- **Check each variant's band for single-colour rows before reading any
  zero.** The scorer should refuse a pair whose band is striped. This run's
  would have.
