## What the vertical axis's 2,088 px are

They are not the size or shape of a low-byte offset.

| `_v_lo00` against `Y8_v` | px | inside the quads | in the seam bands | on a vertical checker edge |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| silicon | 2,088 | 1,976 | 658 | 764 |
| hakuX (full 16-bit value) | 770 | 658 | 0 | 254 |

- **The size.** Forcing the low bytes to 0x00 changes each texel's full value
  by 0x52/65535, about 0.1%. On hakuX, which reads the full value, that moves
  770 px, most at checker edges.
- **The shape.** Silicon's vertical axis moves 2,088 px, the same order, and a
  third of them sit on vertical edges. A low-byte read moves tens of thousands
  (42,508 on the horizontal axis).
- **So the vertical offset reads a value dominated by the high byte,** most
  likely the full value, with the low byte adding a fraction. Whether silicon
  uses the full value or the high byte plus some other small term is not
  separated here.

## What it means

- **#10's seam effect is explained.** A Y16 bump texel's **low byte drives the
  horizontal offset** (the m00 term). Across a byte-replicated seam, 0x5252 to
  0x5353, the filtered value's low byte sweeps about 200 values, and the
  horizontal lookup sweeps with it. That is #10's "horizontal cell-parity
  flip". Outside the seam the two bytes are equal, so Y16 matches Y8.
- **For a hakuX fix,** in the bump path of `psh.c`, for a Y16 source:
  - the horizontal offset should take the filtered value's low byte, as an
    8-bit value;
  - the vertical offset should take the high byte, or the full value.
  - The fix belongs to whichever lane the board assigns.
