# Prior lanes on the draw/PFIFO thread's time and waits -- digest for lane.halo1016

Scope: what existing instruments and levers bear on "what is the PFIFO/draw
thread doing while off-CPU with a backlog" (Halo CE cinematic plateau, 8-19fps,
vCPU 40% idle on NOP-callback waits, fifoskew drain n=0 / backlog 150-230KB).
Read in full unless noted "(skim)". Citations are `lane/file:approx-line-or-section`.

## 3. "Do not repeat" / dead-end items (consolidated, deduplicated)

- Do not read hakuX-phase's R/X split on the Nova as render-time/transfer-time: both timestamps sit inside the Vulkan render pass; GMEM replays the pass per tile so R=last tile, X=everything earlier (binning+earlier tiles+loads/stores). Not a transfer cost. (flip474)
- Do not trust `limits.timestampPeriod` on this Adreno: it reads 1.573x too fast. Fit against the CPU clock (`o4clock.py`) and keep a drift check before quoting any GPU-ms number from the Nova. (flip474)
- Do not read median of timing reps without looking at the rows -- two-humped distributions hide in medians. (flip474)
- Do not attribute a vCPU/PFIFO lock wait to the phase where the wait *began* -- it lasts until the method returns; read hakuX-phase/surf413 for what the method actually spent. (flip474)
- Do not assume "same call site" means "same fix reaches it" -- check which branch of the function each title/path takes (forza414's fix does not reach DOA's branch of `download_surface_complete_deferred`; O1-O4 do not reach AUF/Blinx's path either). (flip474)
- Do not treat a relieved vCPU/PFIFO wait as a guaranteed fps win ("vcpusleep" shape): the freed thread can race into a different lock (pgraph.lock doubling in pfifowait1009), or the sleep can be layered (vcpuwait433's 0.5ms win vs 2.8ms predicted). **Expect layering on Halo too** -- removing the first-named wait may expose a second one, not a fps win, on the first try.
- Do not read `[tlb68] rdus`/`rdous` as surface-download wait time -- it is `tlb_reset_dirty` time. (vcpuwait433)
- Do not read a fps-overlay number as guest fps on a route frame -- it can read 59 while `hakuX-pace` says 27-31. (vcpuwait433)
- Do not infer menu/profile state from `titlestate.py show`'s "profile ... save X" -- read the actual menu frame. (vcpuwait433)
- Do not compare A/B fps without looking at the captured shots: scene variation (a KO replay, a boss fight) can dominate a 1-2fps "win". (flip474)
- `RenderCommandSnapshot`/`RCMD_DRAW`/`pgraph_vk_submit_worker_init` exist in the tree already (compiled only under `#ifndef NDEBUG`, never wired up, never called) -- do not rediscover this as a new design; it was designed once and explicitly not built because the floor-cost case measured out negative (drawrec1010 Addendum1 + frame-pacing-and-parallelism.md sec.2).
- The NULLREC "recorder is useless" finding was measured on a title at its vblank floor (33-34ms/frame, 96% v2). **Halo's plateau is not at a vblank floor -- it is 2-6x slower than 30fps.** Re-examine whether the recorder-thread conclusion actually transfers before citing it as settled for Halo.
- fifoskew's parser must be checked with `fifo_skew_report.py --selftest` against the exact `pfifo.c` format string -- a silent regex mismatch reads as "a soak that emitted nothing," which looks exactly like a failed run and cost two false negatives previously. (guest-pgraph-skew.md)
- `[rwait526]`'s wake-up-latency safety leg (K1) has only ever been validated on vblank-capped titles (Crimson Skies, Otogi, both locked to 29fps in the tested window). It has never been checked on an uncapped/GPU-bound title -- which is exactly Halo's plateau shape. Do not assume `HAKUX_RENDER_WAIT=block`'s wake-up cost is harmless there without checking p99 wait time against Halo's own frame budget.

## 4. Conclusions on the #474/#488 guest-waits-on-callback mechanism

- The mechanism (flip474 sec.13-15, `[cblat]`): AUF and Blinx are idle until the PGRAPH ERROR interrupt that `NV097_NO_OPERATION`-with-parameter raises. Built `[cblat]` specifically to answer "is the callback late because the guest's NOP is unacknowledged (`pnop`), or because the PFIFO thread is still dispatching the frame's draws (`mdraw`)?"
- **Result differed by title.** AUF: `shared` (dispatch overlapping the callback wait) is ~100%; of its 50.1ms mdraw time, 19.3ms is a *nested* deferred-download wait (`dl`, i.e. cdef/surfupd), 11.3ms is flip pacing, ~31ms is draws' own cost. Blinx: `shared` 78%, but `dl`/`fin`/`msema`/`pflip` are all near-zero (<0.5ms/frame combined) -- "None of O1, #488, flip pacing or forza414's deferral has more than 0.5ms/frame to take. What sets Blinx's callback is the PFIFO thread's draw dispatch: 36.8 of a 62.5ms frame."
- The `pnop`-dominant hypothesis (S1) **HELD for AUF, was REFUTED for Blinx** (`pnop` was Blinx's *smallest* category, `mdraw` its largest). This is the single most important prior finding for Halo: **the same "guest waits on NOP callback" surface symptom (#474/#488's AUF/Blinx mechanism, which is exactly the Halo picture's own candidate #1) had two completely different underlying causes on two different titles.** Do not assume Halo's plateau is "waiting_for_nop" just because the symptom (busy periods starting on PGRAPH ERROR) matches -- that symptom is also consistent with mdraw (still-dispatching) on the evidence above.
- Against real silicon: kick-to-handled is 13-14.5us flat regardless of frame complexity on real hardware; on hakuX the gap *grows with queued draws* (19.6ms Blinx, 93.5ms AUF at p50) -- i.e. this is a **backlog-depth effect**, not a fixed per-callback cost. Halo's picture (backlog 150-230KB, ~1000-1200 binds/draws per plateau frame vs ~250 at 30fps) fits this shape closely.
- "The next lever for both [AUF and Blinx] is the per-draw cost of SET_BEGIN_END on the PFIFO thread... This instrument ends there" -- `[cblat]` was never extended past this. No lane has built a per-draw-method-cost breakdown *inside* `mdraw` beyond drawrec1010's census (which measures CPU cost, not what fraction is itself blocked on a GPU wait embedded in a draw, e.g. `pgraph_vk_finish(FLIP_STALL)` fired mid-batch).
- vcpuwait433's independent finding (different title, Tron, different symptom -- vCPU sleep not draw-thread backlog) converges on the same underlying culprit shape: the PFIFO thread asleep in `wait_frame_submitted <- pgraph_vk_finish <- pgraph_vk_process_pending_reports`, holding pfifo.lock, during a STALLED finish that runs only once DMA_GET==DMA_PUT (i.e. once the PFIFO thread has already drained the backlog). This is a *different* wait than #474/#488's NOP-callback wait but lives in the same function family and the same thread.

## 5. What applies to Halo's picture above -- ranked

**Instruments to switch on for a Nova run of the Halo cinematic (no code changes needed, all already built):**

1. **`[cblat]`** (flip474, perflog build) -- directly splits "waiting_for_nop" from "still dispatching" for the plateau's NOP-callback busy periods. This is the instrument built for exactly this symptom, and it already falsified the naive "symptom=pnop" reading once (Blinx). Highest priority.
2. **`HAKUX_PROBE_WAITS=1`** (drawrec1010, always-safe) -- times every GPU fence wait on the PFIFO thread inside `pgraph_vk_finish`, by reason (STALLED/FLIP_STALL/SURFACE_DOWN) and rotation-fence wait, with a 0/1/2/3/4+ per-draw histogram. Distinguishes "pgraph_vk_finish GPU syncs" (one of the picture's named candidates) from everything else, cheaply.
3. **`fifoskew`+`vblphase`** (always on) -- already partially read in the picture (drain n=0, backlog 150-230KB); re-read with `fifo_skew_report.py --selftest` run first to rule out a parser mismatch.
4. **`HAKUX_GPUXFR=1`** (gpunonrender) -- if (1)+(2) show time inside a `pgraph_vk_finish`/fence wait, this splits what GPU-side category (download/s2t/surf_up/ctrl) that wait is actually for.
5. **Apply the 1.573x timestamp correction** to any hakuX-phase R/X/GPU number before trusting it (flip474 sec.17) -- this alone changed a prior lane's whole conclusion about where excess time was.
6. If 1-4 leave an unexplained residual: run **`capture_offcpu.sh`/`waitsite.py`** (vcpuwait433) against the **PFIFO thread's own tid**, not the vCPU's -- this is the generic off-CPU-with-holder-attribution tool and nothing in this reading list has pointed it at the draw thread itself yet. It is the most direct way to answer "what is the draw thread doing while off-CPU" if the targeted instruments above don't add up.

**Parked levers that act on this mechanism, in order of plausible relevance:**

1. **Per-draw SET_BEGIN_END cost inside mdraw** -- named but never built by flip474 as "the next lever for both [AUF and Blinx]." If `[cblat]` shows Halo is mdraw-dominant like Blinx, this unbuilt instrument is the next step, not a known fix.
2. **O1-O3 (lazy/gated/off-thread completion of the deferred-download/cdef wait)** -- flip474's family, real but small once GPU-timestamp-corrected (~1ms bound, not 18ms), and does NOT reach AUF/Blinx's code path (different branch). Check which branch Halo's plateau takes before assuming this applies.
3. **`HAKUX_S2T_CVT=1`** (fps1015) -- if Halo's cinematic samples a previously-rendered 16-bit surface as a texture (worth checking `check_surface_to_texture_compatiblity` refusals in logcat), this is a built, measured, large-win lever already default-off for an unrelated reason (vk/draw.c regression WAITING on its owner). Cheap to test in isolation even while that regression is open, since the pixel-suite failure is in a different, unrelated test (`Texture_shadow_comparator`).
4. **`HAKUX_RENDER_WAIT=block`** is already shipped default and already measured not to help GPU-bound/uncapped titles (never tested on one) -- if Halo's plateau shows large `[rwait526]` wait times with p99 climbing, this is the one shipped lever whose safety case does not yet cover Halo's shape; worth a targeted re-check rather than a new fix.
5. **Recorder thread / `RenderCommandSnapshot`->`RCMD_DRAW`** -- explicitly DO NOT BUILD per drawrec1010, but that verdict was measured at a vblank floor, not at a 2-6x-below-cap plateau. Do not re-propose without re-measuring the floor-vs-wait-cost tradeoff specifically on a plateau frame.
6. Lock-release levers (pfifowait1009's `HAKUX_PFIFOWAIT`, vcpuwait433's lock-free `user_read`) -- both are about a *different* thread's (vCPU's) wait on PFIFO-held locks, not the PFIFO thread's own off-CPU time; relevant only if Halo's analysis flips to "the vCPU is the bottleneck," and both carry known regression risk (pixel correctness, layered waits) if reapplied naively.
