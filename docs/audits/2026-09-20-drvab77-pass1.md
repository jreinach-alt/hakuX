**0 HIGH. 5 MEDIUM. 4 LOW.**

The diff is +863/−0 over five new files, all under `docs/lanes/drvab77/`. No
source file, no golden, no prediction, no board file — so nothing here can
move a pixel, and the whole surface is an argument plus the four scripts that
produce it. The question an audit of that shape has to answer is not "does it
compile" but **does the published verdict follow from the runs**, so I
re-derived it from the result dirs rather than reading the tables.

**It does.** Every rate in the PR body, in `NOTES.md` and in `compare_arms.py`
reproduces to the decimal, on all ten runs (this lane's six and
lane.diagsoak77's four), and the verdict holds under both the originally
registered band and the widened one. No MEDIUM below changes the answer.
What they change is whether the record can be checked: three of them are
places where a stated number is not the quantity it claims to be, and two are
checks that cannot fail on the case they were written for.

## What I checked and found correct, so remediation does not re-derive it

* **All ten rates reproduce, from the PPMs.** I ran this lane's own
  `score_arms.py` over the six result dirs and lane.diagsoak77's four, at
  whole frame and at `--region 0,288,640,480`. Every one of the twenty numbers
  matches `NOTES.md` P1, P5, R0 and R3 and `compare_arms.py`'s tables exactly:
  C3-C6 at 9.6/6.8/16.4/6.6 and 27.4/30.1/23.3/22.4, and the six arms at
  9.5/5.4/5.1/8.4/4.8/9.2 and 23.8/16.3/15.4/22.9/20.2/23.7. The driver string
  read off each dump matches the arm it is filed under in all ten cases,
  including `Qualcomm Technologies Inc. Adreno` on both stock runs. The
  hand-transcription in `compare_arms.py` is clean.
* **The arithmetic in `compare_arms.py` is right.** `sd` 3.97 / 4.71, means
  7.45 / 6.75 / 7.00, largest between-arm gap 0.70, smallest within-arm
  difference 3.30, 1.98x against 2.48x — all reproduce. The sensitivity
  identity is correct: with two runs per arm the se of a 2-run mean is
  `sd/√2` and the se of a difference of two such means is `sd`, so `2·sd`
  = 7.9 (88% of the T30 mean) is the right interval and it is stated as a
  bound rather than as a measurement.
* **The post-hoc band widening is disclosed AND inert on the verdict.** R0
  widens `R_full` from 6.6–16.4 to 5.4–16.4 and `R_lower` from 22.4–30.1 to
  16.3–30.1 after seeing `t30-2`. That is the move a pre-registration exists
  to stop, and R0 argues it is admissible because the widening number is the
  control's and it makes a positive result harder. I checked the argument is
  not load-bearing: under the **original** P1/P5 bands, no arm has both runs
  outside on the same side either (`t26`: 5.1 out, 8.4 in; `stock`: 4.8 out,
  9.2 in; `t30`: 5.4 out, 9.5 in — and on `R_lower`, 15.4/22.9, 20.2/23.7,
  16.3/23.8). The registered rule returns the same null on the registered
  band. Remediation must not narrow the band back; it only needs to record
  that the verdict does not depend on which band is used.
* **The instruments this lane imports did not move under it.** `score_arms.py`
  imports `stipple_classify.py` from lane.diagsoak77 and `load_frames` from
  `docs/testing/galleon_flash_rate.py`. The branch is 16 commits behind master
  and I checked `git log HEAD..origin/master` over both paths: empty. The six
  runs were scored with the same code master has, so being behind did not
  stale the numbers.
* **`load_frames` is 1:1 with its paths** (`mosaic=None`), so `len(rows)` is
  `len(paths)` and the printed `imgs` really is the rate's denominator, as the
  docstring claims. `flagged_frames`'s `idx[r["frame"]]` indirection is
  correct: `ordered_frames` and the `sorted(..., key=int)` in `score()` agree
  on order.
* **`window_audit.py` reproduces R1's table exactly** — 4 runs, 0 foreign, the
  same four ids and clock times.
* **`tests_session_header.py` passes all four cases**, and the two mutants
  R2 names do fail as claimed (the old one-line reader fails case 1; dropping
  `t == "session"` fails case 3).
* **The fix in `session_header` cannot fabricate a header.** Accumulation is
  reset per file, never across a parsed record, and a truncated session line
  followed by draw records cannot re-parse: the first `"` in `{"t":"draw"` closes
  the dangling string and leaves `t":...` unparseable at a fixed prefix
  position, so adding lines never helps. G2's void is intact.
* **The claim that the artifact fires on stock (R4)** is the finding most
  likely to route future work, and it is the one least exposed to everything
  below: it needs only that the stock arm ran the Adreno driver (derived from
  the dump, verified above) and that its rate is non-zero (4.8 and 9.2).

## MEDIUM

### M1. The gap detector fires on every run by construction, and truncates away the one anomaly it exists for

`score_arms.py:114` — `gaps = [(a, b) for a, b in zip(idx, idx[1:]) if b != a + 1]`
`score_arms.py:172-174` — prints `o["gaps"][:4]`.

The file's headline claim is that it *"FIXES THE FRAME ORDER"* and reports a
gap, because *"`stipple_classify.py`'s local baseline is a window over
NEIGHBOURS, and neighbours that are 90 frames apart are not a baseline"*. But a
`cap200` dump keeps roughly one image every 9–10 frames — P1 note 1 says so
itself — so `b != a + 1` is true of **almost every adjacent pair in every
run**. The warning is always on, which is why `NOTES.md` never reports it
firing: it carries no information.

The cost is that the genuine anomalies are invisible inside it. Per-run
spacing, measured:

| run | imgs | median Δ | **max Δ** | Δ > 2×median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C3 / C4 / C5 / C6 (the band) | 73-76 | 9-10 | **15-16** | **0** |
| `t30-1` | 63 | 10 | **43** | 3 |
| `t30-2` | 92 | 7 | 15 | 1 |
| `t26-1` | 78 | 9 | **38** | 1 |
| `t26-2` | 83 | 7 | **53** | 5 |
| `stock-1` / `stock-2` | 84 / 76 | 9 | 15 | 0 |

Three of this lane's six runs have a hole 4–6× their own median spacing.
**None of the four runs that define the band does.** And the operator never
saw it, because `[:4]` shows the first four gaps and the first four are always
the routine ones. Run as committed:

```
1789845130-diagsoak77-C3-244939   ... 73  7  9.6
    GAP in frame numbering: [(4, 13), (13, 23), (23, 32), (32, 41)] -- ...
1789940132-drvab77-t30-1-1103129  ... 63  6  9.5
    GAP in frame numbering: [(9, 18), (18, 27), (27, 37), (37, 46)] -- ...
```

C3 (max hole 15) and `t30-1` (max hole 43) print the same reassuring line.

**Failure scenario.** `t30-1` drops ~13 images relative to the band's cadence,
clustered in three holes, one of 43 frames. Across such a hole the classifier's
±10-neighbour median is taken over roughly 400 frames of wall time instead of
~190, so the smooth animation trend the local baseline exists to remove is
removed less well, and a frame riding a ramp can clear the 1.45× bar — which
is precisely the failure `stipple_classify.py` was written to stop
`galleon_flash_rate.py` from making. I cannot show it moved a number (the
gappy runs are 9.5, 5.1 and 8.4 — no clean direction), and I am not claiming
it did. I am claiming the lane pooled four evenly-sampled band runs with two
irregular ones into the `sd` that the whole sensitivity statement rests on,
without ever seeing that they differed.

**Remedy.** Flag on spacing anomaly, not on non-adjacency: report `max Δ`
against the run's own median Δ, and print the largest gaps rather than the
first four. Then state the six-run spacing profile in `NOTES.md` and say
whether the rates are stable under it.

### M2. R3's G1 evidence mixes whole-frame and `R_lower` numbers, and the mixture voids a band run

`NOTES.md:325-326` — *"All pass **G1** (median HF 1.44-1.76 against 1.24-2.14;
median brightness 29.7-35.8 against 29.1-39.3)."*

G1 is registered in P3 against P1's **whole-frame** medians (*"From P1, a run
of this spec has median HF `1.65-1.71` and median frame brightness
`33.9-34.4`"*), and R0 reports whole-frame values for the T30 arms (1.758 /
1.635, 34.2 / 33.6), which I reproduce exactly. R3's range is not that
quantity. Re-derived per run:

| | whole frame (what G1 bounds) | `R_lower` |
|---|---|---|
| median HF, six arms | **1.635 – 1.758** | 1.438 – 1.691 |
| median brightness, six arms | **33.6 – 35.8** | 29.7 – 33.7 |

R3's `1.44` is `t26-2`'s ***`R_lower`*** median HF (1.438) and its `29.7` is
`t30-2`'s ***`R_lower`*** median brightness; the upper ends, `1.76` and `35.8`,
are whole-frame (`t30-1`, `stock-1`). One range, two quantities, and the lower
ends come from a region G1's bound was never derived for.

**Failure scenario.** As written the record says a run came within 2% of
G1's brightness floor (29.7 against 29.1) and 16% of its HF floor, so a reader
concludes the content guard nearly fired and the arms differed in scene. The
truth is the opposite — whole-frame content across all ten runs spans 7.5% in
HF and 6.8% in brightness, tighter than G1 needed, and the arms saw the same
scene. Worse in the other direction: a reader who takes R3 at face value and
applies the 29.1 floor to `R_lower` medians **voids `C6` at 27.9** — one of the
four runs that define the band the entire verdict is measured against.

**Remedy.** State G1 per run on the quantity it bounds, as R0 does. The
six whole-frame pairs are above and every one passes with room; the
conclusion does not change.

### M3. The sentence that applies the pre-registered rule miscounts the excursions

`NOTES.md:349-351` — *"On `R_full` the band is 5.4-16.4 over six T30 runs and
every one of the six arms' runs is inside it but for `t26-1` at 5.1 — whose
partner is at 8.4, so the arm's range overlaps and the rule is not met."*

`stock-1` is **4.8**, which is below 5.4 and further outside the band than
`t26-1`. Two of the six arm runs fall below the floor, not one. (The parallel
`R_lower` sentence is correct: at band 16.3–30.1, `t26-1`'s 15.4 really is the
only excursion.)

**Failure scenario.** This is the one sentence in the lane that applies P4's
registered rule to the data, so it is the sentence an auditor or a downstream
reader checks the verdict against. As written it says one arm run strayed
below the control band; two did, and both non-T30 arms produced one. The
verdict is unaffected — `stock-2` at 9.2 is inside, so `stock` does not meet
"both runs outside on the same side" — but the pattern the miscount hides is
the one R0 flagged and then stopped tracking: every new batch of runs pushes
the low end of this band down (16.4→ band, then 5.4, then 5.1 and 4.8), which
bears directly on R5's "effects below the interval" caveat.

**Remedy.** *"Two runs fall below the floor — `t26-1` at 5.1 and `stock-1` at
4.8 — and neither arm's partner does (8.4, 9.2), so no arm meets the rule."*

### M4. `window_audit.py` asks "finished inside the window"; exposure is "overlapped the window"

`window_audit.py:44` — `if a <= mt <= b:`, where `mt` is the result
directory's mtime.

A request the dispatcher claimed for the thor **before** a swap window opened
and that finished **after** T30 was restored ran its entire measurement on the
swapped driver, and its mtime is in neither window, so it is in neither the
listing nor the `foreign` count. That is the largest-exposure case and the
only one the check cannot see. The start time is not missing — it is the
epoch prefix of every result id, and `request.json` carries `queued_utc`.

I re-ran the audit as an overlap test (`start <= b and end >= a`) over all 966
result dirs: **the answer for these two windows is unchanged** — 4 runs, all
this lane's, nothing missed. So R1's "these two windows were clean" stands and
nothing in this PR is contaminated. The defect is in the instrument, and R6
hands that instrument to the next driver experiment as the check to run.

**Failure scenario.** The next lane swaps a driver, a foreign arms-job run
straddles the window, `window_audit.py` prints `0 not this lane's`, and that
lane's A/B verdict is measured on a driver it never asked for — the exact
"arm B inherits arm A's independent variable" failure R1 names.

**Remedy.** Three lines: parse the queue epoch from the id, test overlap, and
say in the docstring that a run with no parseable start is listed rather than
dropped.

### M5. Case 4 does not pin the bound it says it pins — the unbounded mutant passes all four cases

`tests_session_header.py:83-86` — *"A truncated header ... Unbounded
accumulation over a huge file is the other way to 'fix' case 1; this says the
answer is still None rather than a scan of the whole dump."*

I built that third mutant — `MAX_HEADER_LINES` and its `break` deleted from
`score_arms.session_header`, nothing else changed, in a scratch symlink tree —
and ran the committed test file against it:

```
PASS multi-line driver recovers
PASS single-line driver still works
PASS no session record stays None
PASS truncated header stays None
ALL PASS   EXIT=0
```

Case 4 asserts the *answer* (`None`), and the unbounded reader also answers
`None`, because a truncated session line followed by draw records never
re-parses no matter how many lines you add. The bound is not under test.

**Failure scenario.** Someone simplifies the loop by dropping the bound; the
test file stays green and R2's "neither mutant passes both" still reads true;
`session_header` then concatenates an entire framedump JSONL into `buf` and
calls `json.loads` on it once per line. Measured on a synthetic dump with an
unreadable header: 0.000 s bounded vs 0.106 s and 1.55 MB resident at 20k
lines, growing linearly with the dump. Not a crash — a silent removal of the
one protection the docstring is loudest about.

**Remedy.** Assert the bound, not the answer: have `session_header` record how
many lines it consumed (it already returns `_header_lines` on success) and have
case 4 assert the reader stopped at `MAX_HEADER_LINES`, or give case 4 enough
lines that an unbounded scan is observably different.

## LOW

### L1. `score_arms.py` documents a void rule it does not implement

`score_arms.py:15` — *"A run whose header names a driver the caller did not
ask for is void, not renamed"*, and P2 repeats it as *"void and retaken"*.
There is no expected-driver argument and no voiding code anywhere in the diff.
The driver is printed into a column for a human to compare, and
`compare_arms.py` — where the verdict arithmetic actually happens — carries
no driver field at all, only 24 hand-transcribed rates keyed by **typed** arm
names. The practice was sound here (I verified all ten driver strings against
their arms), but the file states a machine guard where it has a human one.
Either implement `--expect-driver` or say "printed for comparison".

### L2. The `galleon_flash_rate` import works only as a side effect of the line above it

`score_arms.py:34-38` inserts its own directory and `../diagsoak77`, but never
`docs/testing`. `from galleon_flash_rate import load_frames` resolves only
because `from stipple_classify import classify` on the previous line inserts
`../../testing` into `sys.path` at import time. Any import reorder — isort,
ruff, a tidy-up — breaks the file. Insert the path it actually needs.

### L3. `NOTES.md:324`'s G2 range excludes a run in its own table

*"All pass **G2** (76-92 images)"*, two lines above a table whose first row is
`t30-1` at **63**. G2 still passes (bar is 60, and R0 says "63 and 92"
correctly), but the R3 summary range is over five of the six runs.

### L4. `window_audit.py`'s inputs are unverifiable by a later reader

`window_audit.py:19-27` hardcodes `/home/justin/hakux-work/dispatch/results`
and the two windows as bare epoch literals *"recorded at the time"*, with no
artifact behind them. The script is the audit of an out-of-band action, so the
one thing it cannot derive is the one thing that matters. Point the bounds at
`swap_driver.sh`'s own log or a recorded marker file, and take `D` from an
env var with that default.

## Severity notes

No HIGH. Nothing in this diff executes on a device, in the emulator or in CI;
the five MEDIUMs are defects in an argument and in the offline tools that
produced it, and I checked each against the runs before filing it. I dropped
two candidate findings after checking:

* **"The arms saw different scenes, and G1's ±25% bound is too wide to catch
  it"** — this is what M2's stated range implies, and it is false. Whole-frame
  content across all ten runs spans 7.5% (HF) and 6.8% (brightness); the
  content regime really is as repeatable as P1 says. M2 is a reporting
  defect, not a confound.
* **"The band widening lets the null through"** — checked and false; the
  registered bands give the same verdict (above).

`preflight.sh`'s coverage gate failing on **#200** is correctly diagnosed in
R7 as the board's and not this lane's. I did not re-run it.
