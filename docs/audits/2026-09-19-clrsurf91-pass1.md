## What I checked and found correct, so remediation does not re-derive it

* **The upload side is bit-identical, as claimed.** Old
  `!cb || (upload && (buffer_dirty || mem_dirty))`, new
  `upload && (!cb || buffer_dirty || mem_dirty)`. When `upload` is true the
  two reduce to `!cb || buffer_dirty || mem_dirty` term for term. When
  `upload` is false the old form is `!cb` and the new is `false`. **The only
  behavioural delta in the whole gate is "download with no binding", which is
  exactly what the PR says it is.** No third case exists.
* **`gate_open` has no other consumer that could shift.** Its only uses are
  `if (gate_open)` at `:3545` and the `gate_open` argument to `surf92_probe`,
  and that call sits inside `if (upload)`, so the probe's recorded semantics
  are unchanged. Checked by reading every occurrence in the function.
* **No NULL dereference is introduced.** The local `SurfaceBinding *surface`
  is declared inside the `if (gate_open)` block and every use of it —
  `trace_fn`, `bind_surface`, the compatibility arms — is inside that block.
  The tail's new `drawn` is guarded before `download_surface_deferred`.
* **The tail's "the drawn surface is still downloaded elsewhere" claim is
  true, and address-keyed rather than binding-keyed.** I did not take this on
  the PR's word. `pgraph_vk_download_surfaces_in_range_if_dirty()`
  (`surface.c:428`) walks **all three** lists — `r->surfaces`,
  `r->shelved_surfaces`, `r->invalid_surfaces` — matching on
  `check_surface_overlaps_range()` and on `surface->draw_dirty`, with no
  reference to `r->color_binding`/`r->zeta_binding`. Both callers of the
  download form of `pgraph_vk_surface_update()` are followed by a range
  download over the memory they are about to read: `blit.c:446` by
  `blit.c:558`/`:560` (src and dest), `blit.c:861` by `blit.c:900`. Texture
  (`texture.c:1863`) and vertex (`vertex.c:50`) fetch go through the same
  function, and the flip/CPU-access paths key on `surface->draw_dirty`
  (`:1690`, `:1728`) rather than on a binding. **So retiring
  `pg_surface->draw_dirty` without downloading does not orphan a drawn
  image**: the per-surface flag survives and the consumers that matter find
  it by address. This was the most likely way the fix could be wrong and it
  is not wrong.
* **Not clearing `pg_surface->buffer_dirty` on a declined download is safe on
  its own terms.** A still-set `buffer_dirty` makes the next upload pass
  `unbind_surface()` and then re-resolve in `update_surface_part(d, true, …)`
  — the same binding it would have had. (It does widen the binding-less
  window, which is why it appears again under M1.)
* **The arm scaffolding is genuinely self-cancelling, verified not asserted.**
  `git diff aec524681e b86517efe7` is exactly the fix (+152/−27 in
  `surface.c`), i.e. `b86517efe7` withdraws precisely what `aec524681e`
  restored, and `git diff --stat origin/master...HEAD` carries one source
  file. Both prediction refs `aec524681e` (a_ref) and `7980d1caa2` (b_ref)
  are ancestors of the tip (`git log origin/master..HEAD`), so the merge did
  not un-ancestor them.
* **The M3 tension the PR raises against itself is resolved the right way,
  and I am saying so here as it asked.** An arm of `master -> master+fix`
  would move no leg, because the precondition (`zeta_binding` absent across a
  download) is what #88's policy creates; an arm that cannot move is an inert
  control and discharges no blocker. Carrying the policy constant in both
  arms and varying only the fix is the measurement that exists. **I do not
  raise this as a finding and pass 2 should not re-open it.**
* **The prediction's eleven keys are `Suite_dir/TestName` with one slash and
  underscores in the suite**, and `disc.suites` names all three directories
  the keys come from, so the three-suite disc requirement it states for
  itself is internally consistent. `expect` 3 + `must_not_move` 8 = 11.
* **Arm A is registered as a real check**, with the re-reading to apply if
  304,750 does not reproduce, and the prediction states before any number is
  read that neither 165,447 nor 304,750 is correct and that a third value is
  not a pass. That is the right shape and I have nothing to add to it.
* **The index regeneration did not drop a suite.** `provenance.tests_commit`
  is `91a0de45ca…` on both master and the branch, `suites` is 103 on both,
  `symbols` 951 on both, `gaps` 498 on both. The staleness under M3 is
  emulator-side only.
* **No skip-ci marker anywhere in the eight commit messages.** CI ran: two
  `build` and one `check`, all SUCCESS.

## MEDIUM

### M1 — the declined download consumes the NV2A dirty bits and drops them

`update_surface_part()` test-and-**clears** the `DIRTY_MEMORY_NV2A` bitmap
over the target's page range at `:3475`, before the gate and **regardless of
`upload`**. `mem_dirty` has exactly two consumers, both inside the gate
block: `surface->upload_pending |= mem_dirty` on the compatible-hit path
(`:3747`) and `surface->upload_pending = shelf_stale || mem_dirty` on the
unshelve path (`:3818`).

Before this change, a download with no binding opened the gate, and those two
lines therefore ran: a CPU write to that range was recorded on the binding
the call resolved. After this change `gate_open` is false on every download,
so on a binding-less download the bits are cleared and `mem_dirty` is
computed and then used by nothing at all.

**Failure scenario.** Guest CPU writes the colour surface's VRAM range. A
download call for that part runs with the binding absent (`blit.c:446`, or
`blit.c:861`, reached via `pgraph_vk_surface_update(d, false, …)` whenever
`pg->surface_color.draw_dirty` is set) and consumes the dirty bits for the
range. The next upload pass finds the binding still absent, opens the gate on
the `!current_binding` term, and lands on the compatible-hit path at `:3747`
or the unshelve path at `:3818`. `mem_dirty` is now **false**, because this
call's test-and-clear found nothing left to find. `upload_pending` stays
false, `surface->initialized` stays true, and the host image is used with the
guest's write never uploaded — which is verbatim the failure the comment at
`:3806-3814` says the unshelve test exists to prevent ("Taking the image
as-is in that case put stale pixels back over the newer ones").

Two things bound this, and I weighed both before settling on MEDIUM rather
than HIGH. The same loss already happens on a download whose binding is
*present* (gate closed, `mem_dirty` discarded), so the class is not new — the
diff extends it to the binding-absent case. And on master the binding-absent
case is, by the lane's own model, close to unreachable. But that is the
lane's model of *today*: the precondition here is identical to the
precondition the fix targets, so it is frequent in exactly the arm being
measured and becomes frequent on master the moment #88's policy re-lands —
which this PR argues it has unblocked. The same change should not both make
the state common and start dropping the one flag that state carries.

Not clearing `pg_surface->buffer_dirty` on the declined path (see above)
amplifies this: `buffer_dirty` now persists across the download, so the next
upload pass unbinds first, and if that part's write is not enabled the
binding stays absent into the following download. That is the loop in which
the scenario above repeats.

**Remediation direction** (not a prescription): make the declined download
stop consuming state it will not use — compute the `mem_dirty` scan only when
`upload`, so the bits survive to the next upload — or carry `mem_dirty`
forward explicitly on the declined branch. Whichever is chosen, say in the
comment what the declined path is allowed to consume, because the comment at
`:3486-3532` currently discusses only the binding.

### M2 — `dl91_probe`'s own comment documents an `addr=` field the probe does not emit

`:309-312` states, as one of the three things the probe establishes:

> it is placed after the gate, so a binding the gate would once have invented
> is absent by construction; `addr=` is therefore the address that download
> would have gone to, **which is the number worth having**.

The probe (`:329`) takes `(PGRAPHState const *pg, bool color)` — no address —
and its log line (`:349`) is
`"[dl91] frame=%d declined=%lu f_declined=%lu part=%s"`. There is no `addr=`.
The call site at `:3903` has `target.vram_addr` in scope, exactly as
`surf91_overlap_probe`'s call site does; `surf91_overlap_probe` takes
`hwaddr addr` and prints `overlap=0x%08…`, and its comment goes to the
trouble of explaining which address it deliberately does *not* print. The
contrast is what makes this an omission rather than a choice.

**Failure scenario.** `dl91_probe` is the lane's only instrument for a fix
that is a removal — falsifier (5) reads its counter from the arm log rather
than from the score. Whoever reads that log, having been told by the code
that `addr=` is the number worth having, will grep for it, find no such
field, and either conclude the declines were not address-attributable or
re-run the arm to get it. The counter legs still work; the attribution the
comment promises cannot be performed, and it is the one that would separate
"declined a download that would have gone to the wrong address" from
"declined a download that would have been harmless" — which is precisely the
limit `:298-303` says the counter alone cannot settle.

**Remediation:** pass `target.vram_addr` and print it, or delete the sentence.
One parameter either way.

### M3 — the regenerated `nv2a_index.json` is behind master and is the merge conflict

`git merge-tree --write-tree origin/master HEAD` reports one conflict, and it
is this file:

```
CONFLICT (content): Merge conflict in docs/testing/nv2a_index.json
```

GitHub agrees: `mergeable: CONFLICTING`, `mergeStateStatus: DIRTY`. The PR is
not foldable as it stands, which by itself would send it back.

The content is the reason, not just the mechanics. Against master's tip the
branch's copy has `provenance.emulator_commit` `b86517efe7` (this branch's
own scaffolding commit) where master has `bf0e9b55ba`; `sites` 2833 where
master has 2839; and `hw/xbox/nv2a/pgraph/vk/renderer.c` locs running
**backwards** — 1096→1071, 1297→1272, 1370→1345 — because master's
`renderer.c` has grown 25 lines the branch has not seen. The PR body's
statement that "the counts are unchanged at 951 symbols / 2833 sites / 103
suites" was true against the base it merged on attempt 2 and is no longer
true against master.

**Failure scenario.** A conflict resolved by taking the branch side — the
obvious resolution, since the branch is the side that "regenerated the
index" — lands an index that drops 6 emulator sites and points every
`renderer.c` gap 25 lines off. The index is what briefs and the gap tooling
resolve `loc` from, so the next lane sent to a `renderer.c` line arrives at
the wrong one, and a gap that master currently tracks stops being tracked
with nothing saying it was dropped. Taking master's side instead silently
discards this branch's `surface.c` locs, which are the ones the change
actually moved.

**Remediation:** merge `origin/master`, regenerate the index on the merged
tree (same `tests_root`/`tests_commit` — `91a0de45ca…`, which is what master
carries), and confirm `sites` is ≥ 2839 and `suites` is still 103 before
committing. Re-verify afterwards that `aec524681e` and `7980d1caa2` are still
ancestors — a merge does not un-ancestor them, but say so rather than assume
it, and do **not** rebase.

## LOW

### L1 — "REMOVE ALL FOUR" names five probes, and the block's other half says five

`:178-181` now reads "REMOVE ALL FOUR -- surf92_probe, surf91_overlap_probe
and dl91_probe here, clr89_probe/clr91_probe in vk/draw.c" — that is five
names. `dl91_probe`'s own lifetime line at `:319` says "with the other
**four**", i.e. five in total, which is the correct count and contradicts the
heading four lines of diff away.

The pre-existing text said "REMOVE ALL THREE" over four names, so the
off-by-one is inherited rather than introduced; the diff touched that exact
line, incremented the wrong number, and added an internal contradiction that
was not there before. The block's stated purpose is that "the set still
leaves together", so a miscount in it is a miscount in the only thing it
does.

**Failure scenario.** Whoever closes #91 removes probes by the count the
heading gives, removes four, and ships the fifth — an always-on
`SURF92_LOG`/`__android_log_print` on a per-draw surface path, in a release
build, with nothing left in the tree saying it should have gone.

**Remediation:** make it five, or drop the numeral and let the list be the
list.
