## What was checked and holds

- **The default build compiles the instrument out.** Outside
  `#if defined(__ANDROID__) && NV2A_PERF_LOG`, every hook is a `(void)`
  macro. `cbl_nop` is `0 && ...`, and the short circuit means
  `pending_interrupts` is never read. `cbl_before` and `cbl_jumped` are
  consumed by the `cbl_note_advance` macro's casts, so the build gets no
  unused-variable warning. Desktop and shipping Android builds execute
  nothing new.
- **Locking.** Every `s_cbl` write happens under `pfifo.lock`:
  - `cbl_note_kick` is reached from `user.c`'s DMA_PUT store, which holds
    `pfifo.lock` (`user.c` locks at the top of the write). It is also
    reached only when `current_cpu` is set and DMA_PUT has advanced.
  - The pusher hooks run with `pfifo.lock` held.
  - `pfifo_run_puller` drops `pfifo.lock` around `pgraph_method` and
    retakes it before returning. So `cbl_leave` and `cbl_note_callback`
    run locked, and a kick during the method snapshots the in-progress
    category from its published `cur_t0`, as the comment says.
  - The park's `cbl_enter` comes before both the FIFO_SPIN spin and the
    cond wait, and `cbl_leave` comes after both. Nothing exits between
    them.
- **No phantom callbacks.** A stalled puller returns -1: either
  `pfifo_puller_should_stall` fails, or `PULL0`/`LOW_MARK` does. The pusher
  then breaks before `cbl_note_callback`, so a NOP that was refused and
  retried is timed once, when it is dispatched. `word` in that branch is
  the method's data word, so the recorded parameter is the callback's.
- **`dup` matches the handler.** `pgraph.c`'s Android NOP handler returns
  without raising the interrupt when `NV_PGRAPH_INTR_ERROR` is already
  pending. The instrument reads the same bit before dispatch.
- **Ring retirement.** A linear step retires puts in `(before, after]`. A
  JMP, CALL or RET retires only a put equal to its target. Catching up
  retires everything. At the NOP's dispatch, the step that holds the NOP is
  not yet retired, so `ring[tail]` is the oldest put past the marker. That
  is the kick that published it. When the ring is full, the newest kick is
  dropped, not the oldest, so the attribution stays correct.
- **The `params` buffer cannot overflow.** Each entry is at most 20
  characters (`,` + 8 hex digits + `:` + 10 digits), which fits 8 × 20 + 1.
  The length check runs before the next `snprintf`.
- **Non-overlap.** The split starts at `max(kick, previous callback)`, so
  consecutive callbacks' intervals cannot overlap. `dl` and `fin` are
  capped at the methods' total for the interval.
- **`o4clock.py`'s algebra.** It uses `v = w1 - ce·per` with slope `s`, and
  a true period of `per/(1-s)`. That is right when the GPU counter runs at
  a constant rate.
  - One alternative is a counter that pauses while the GPU is idle. That
    also gives a linear drift, but its whole-run ratio would be lower than
    its in-wait ratio, because the GPU also idles between waits.
  - The NOTES' figures separate the two: 0.636 over the run and 0.642
    (40.5/63.1) inside the wait, and 33.11/0.636 = 52.08 ns is exactly
    19.2 MHz.
  - So the conclusion is supported. It is a Nova-only claim, and the NOTES
    say the Thor was not checked.
- **Predictions.** All four are single-purpose. Their refs are the lane's
  own commits, and their NOTES verdicts report the failed legs (K0, and P4
  as written) and the refuted guess (Blinx S1) as failures. None was
  softened after the run.
- **Self-tests.** `cblread.py --selftest` and `o4read.py --selftest` both
  pass.

## LOW

**L1. `cbl_pct` reports a censored p90 as a value, and NOTES §15 reads it as
one.** `pfifo.c`, `cbl_pct`: each percentile is the upper edge of a 0.25 ms
bin. Every latency over 100 ms lands in the overflow bin, which reports
100.25 ms. NOTES §15 gives AUF's p90 as "100.3" in the table. That is the
overflow bin, so the true p90 is at least 100.25 ms, not 100.3.
- Failure scenario: a later reader compares AUF's p90 before and after a
  fix, sees 100.3 → 100.3, and concludes the tail did not move, when it
  may have moved from 180 ms to 110 ms.
- No verdict in §15 rests on p90: S1 and the levers use span and the
  split. So the damage is limited to the reported figure.
- Fix: print `>=100.25` in `cbl_maybe_dump` (or `cblread.py`), and mark
  the table cell.

**L2. The cost comment understates the instrument's clock reads.** The
header says "a clock read per method dispatch and per park, which the perf
log's own `_meth_t0` already pays."
- `cbl_enter` and `cbl_leave` each call `nv2a_clock_ns()`. That is two
  reads per method and two per park.
- Neither read is shared with `_meth_t0`. It is a separate read inside the
  puller, and the lockless fast path has its own.
- Failure scenario: a later lane reuses a `[cblat]` ref as a perflog fps
  baseline on the strength of the comment. Its method-heavy titles pay 2×
  the clock reads the comment says.
- The P legs (14 vs 14.96 and 16 vs 17.32 gfps) show the cost is small on
  these two titles.
- Fix: correct the comment.

**L3. `o4clock.py` has no self-test and can divide by zero.** It backs the
PR's headline claim, that the Nova's `timestampPeriod` is wrong by 1.573,
which reaches every Nova `hakuX-phase` GPU/R/X figure.
- The script has no `--selftest`: `--selftest` is taken as a result id and
  crashes with FileNotFoundError.
- `sxx` is 0 when every row falls in one 5 s bin. The `len(rows) >= 20`
  guard does not prevent that, so a `--from/--to` window under 5 s raises
  ZeroDivisionError.
- Failure scenario: someone re-runs the fit on a short window to check the
  Thor, and it crashes rather than saying "one bin".
- Fix: require `n >= 3` bins, and add a synthetic self-test with a known
  period (e.g. true 52.08, line 33.11 → slope 0.364).

**L4. The 19.2 MHz correction exists only in lane NOTES and issue comments.**
NOTES §15 says every Nova GPU/R/X figure, slowdown462's and forza414's
included, reads 0.636 of the true value. It names the fix: calibrate
`gpu_ts_period_ns` in `renderer.c`. The lane has not made that change,
which is fair: it is an instrument change outside this lane's scope. But it
has no issue or tracker row of its own, only comments on #474/#462/#425/#414.
- Failure scenario: a lane reads `hakuX-phase GPU=40` on the Nova next week,
  without reading flip474 NOTES, and prices a GPU lever at 40 ms when it
  is 63.
- Recommend: the board files the calibration as its own issue. This is not
  a code defect in this diff.

  three exist in `docs/lanes/flip474/` on master.
- `cblread.py`'s hard-coded `2026-` year only affects timestamps within one
  run, so the relative seconds are right.
- P4 ("FAILS as written") is reported as a failure, with the reason the
  probe's cost cannot be separated in one run. That is the honest reading,
  not a defect.
