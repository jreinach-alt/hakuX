## M1 — CLOSED. The figure is gone from all three places and the replacement re-derives

Pass 1's scenario: the next lane carries "hardware's Y16 offset sweeps at least
~200 byte units" — from the PR body, the investigation and `NOTES.md` — into a
rival sieve, and discards or accepts candidates against a number whose halving
was unlicensed, whose toggles were partly the base texture's, and which named
an excursion a flip count cannot bound.

**The number is gone, and it is gone where it was read.** `grep` over the
branch finds `~200 byte units` in exactly two places, both explicit
withdrawals: `bump-y16-class.md:155` ("An earlier draft of this document
said … both halves of that were wrong") and `NOTES.md:62-73` ("Do not bound a
swing with a flip count"). No surviving assertion, no stale summary line, and
the PR body carries the replacement.

**The replacement is derived once, from a different instrument, and I
re-derived it without the lane's code.** For each row, count the colour
changes of the gold Y8 quad and of the gold Y16 quad across the span; each
change is one step of that image's checker cell index, so the cumulative
movement of `k = idx16 − idx8` is at least `n16 − n8`. That is the total
variation triangle inequality and it is the right shape for the claim. My
measurements, over all four quads:

| quad | max `n16−n8`, cols 44..102 | rows attaining it | min over rows | overlay-polluted rows among the attaining ones |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| g0 b0 | 19 | 150 / 168 | 11 | **0** |
| g0 b1 | 19 | 150 / 168 | 12 | **0** |
| g1 b0 | 19 | 150 / 168 | 11 | **0** |
| g1 b1 | 19 | 150 / 168 | 12 | **0** |

So "19 cells in 150 of the 168 rows of every quad, never below 11 in any row"
is exact, in all four quads and not only the one the tool loops over, and —
importantly, given the new MEDIUM — the rows that attain the bound are exactly
the 150 rows the text overlay does not touch. `(1/32)/(0.3/128) = 13.33` byte
units per cell gives 253, printed as `~250`. The document now states which
quantity is bounded (cumulative movement), says in terms that the excursion is
**not** bounded by this data at all, and tells the reader to size a rival by
the former. `--parity` prints every input to it.

## M3 — NEW, MEDIUM. There is white text drawn inside the quads, and two new sentences call it checker structure

`docs/investigations/bump-y16-class.md:110-111` and `:163-165`;
`docs/testing/bump_y16_class.py:307` and `:352-356`;
`docs/lanes/cloud-10/NOTES.md:33-34` and the PR body.

Each quad contains a block of `(255,255,255,0)` pixels — rows 84..99, columns
44..90 of quad g0 b0 — which renders as glyphs, i.e. the test's own on-screen
label drawn over the quad. It is 360 px in quad g0 b0 and **1,400 px across
the four quads**, and it is present, pixel for pixel, in the gold `Y8`, the
gold `Y16` and our own captures alike. It sits inside the band this whole
investigation measures.

Two statements this remediation added attribute that overlay to the
checkerboard:

* **"the survivors being the rows sitting on a checker boundary"**
  (`md:111`), printed by the tool as "the rows on a checker boundary agree"
  (`py:307`). Measured, in every differing column of every quad: **0 of the
  704 surviving pixels is a checker-boundary row and 704 of 704 are overlay
  pixels.** Quad by quad, survivors / of which overlay: 204/204, 146/146,
  208/208, 146/146. The 90.5% floor is exactly `168 − 16`, the 16 rows the
  glyphs occupy.
* **"the 18 that do are the rows lying on the quad's own vertical checker
  seam"** (`md:163-164`, `py:352-356`), of the rows with a base boundary in
  columns 78..102. Measured: those 18 rows are 84..99 plus 52 and 73. Rows
  84..99 are the glyph rows; only 52 and 73 are the seam at column 83. **16 of
  the 18 are text.**

The truth is stronger than the claim in both cases, which is why it is worth
correcting rather than deleting: **outside the overlay, every single pixel of
every differing column inverts** — the inversion is total in extent as well as
in kind, and the vertical phase is preserved exactly rather than approximately.

**Failure scenario.** The document's own instruction is that "a candidate
mechanism is scored by computing its own `k`". The next lane builds that
scorer against the published structure and gets two false targets. (a) It
takes "152 to 168 of each column's 168 pixels differ, the survivors being the
rows sitting on a checker boundary" as a structural constraint, so a mechanism
that inverts 168 of 168 — which is what the correct mechanism does — scores as
over-inverting, and one that spares boundary rows scores as matching. (b) It
tries to reproduce a base-texture seam feature in columns 78..102 for 18 rows;
no bump mechanism can produce it, because 16 of those rows are a glyph. Both
targets are artifacts of a UI overlay, and this is M2's exact failure mode —
the tool printing a mechanism the data does not support — reintroduced in the
report that replaced it.

**Remediation.** Name the overlay once where the quad geometry is defined
(`py:55-76` and the document's first table), exclude it or say it is excluded
where per-column extent is reported, and correct the two sentences: the
survivors are the glyph pixels, every non-overlay pixel in a differing column
inverts, and the rows with a base boundary in the flat sub-band are the two
seam rows plus the sixteen glyph rows. Nothing else moves: the 22,374 / 1,576
/ 0 / 21,472 table is untouched (the overlay is identical in both images, so
it contributes zero differing pixels), and so is the bound — see the last
column of M1's table, which is the check that says so.

## L7 — NEW, LOW. The bound needs an upper bound on the base's traversal, and cites a lower one

`bump-y16-class.md:129-134`, `bump_y16_class.py:363-370`. "Each colour change
along a row is one step of that image's cell index, so the cumulative movement
of `k` … is at least (Y16's changes − Y8's)." The first half of that difference
is sound — a colour change needs `|Δidx| ≥ 1`, so `n16` is a lower bound on
`TV(idx16)`. The second half needs the opposite: subtracting `TV(idx8)`
requires `n8` to be an *upper* bound on the base's traversal, and a count of
colour changes is prima facie a lower bound — the base could step two cells
between adjacent columns and show no change.

It cannot, here, and I measured it: the gold Y8 row's boundaries sit at columns
2, 7, 13, 18, 23, 28, 34, 39, 44, 49, 55, 60, 65, 70, 76 — minimum gap five
columns, i.e. ≈0.19 cells of base index per column — so the base index cannot
skip a cell and `n8 = TV(idx8)` exactly. The bound stands. This is a
verifiability defect, not a wrong number: the 5.29-column period is published
on the same page, so the fact is there, but the step that uses it is not
stated. One sentence, and the minimum gap is already in reach of `--parity`.

## L8 — NEW, LOW. "every row of the gold Y16 quad has 11 boundaries there"

`bump-y16-class.md:164-165`, `NOTES.md:34` and the PR body. The tool is
